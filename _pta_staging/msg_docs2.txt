docs: review the TPA-QCN proposal by running its physics, not reading it

GRX_AI_Photonics_PTA_TPA_QCN.md proposes an all-optical activation function
from the chi(2) response of an evaporated organic film, and ships about 9,000
lines of Python across seven layers.  Layer 1 is real: a batched, differentiable
coupled-mode solver on torchdiffeq.  So it was extracted and run rather than
read.  Three findings, each reproducible from the scripts added here.

1. The effective-index model cannot phase-match anywhere.  Swept over 38,400
   geometries it never brings n_eff(2w) below n_eff(FF); the minimum gap is
   +0.0427.  The cause is structural: one core index per wavelength and no
   polarisation axis, so the giant negative birefringence the whole TPA-QCN
   premise rests on has nowhere to live.  The document's own reference unit
   carries 547 radians of phase walk-off.  Every transfer function that solver
   has produced is a cascading artefact, and any diffusion model trained on its
   output was fitting physics that cannot express its own target.

2. The nonlinear coupling coefficient is dimensionally inconsistent -- it
   carries m s^-1 W^-1/2 where the coupled-mode equation needs m^-1 W^-1/2 --
   because no effective mode area appears anywhere.  Two independent repairs
   agree: as written it is about 109x too large, which understates the power an
   activation needs by four orders of magnitude.

3. With both repaired, energy per activation is picojoules, not attojoules.
   At the document's own 0.5 mm reference geometry and its assumed 50 pm/V, it
   is 12.5 pJ against a claimed 100 aJ.  With the material's published chi_31
   and the correct tensor element for TE+TE->TM, a 2 mm unit at a 3 dB loss
   budget costs 179 pJ -- roughly 36x worse than the O-E-O path the
   architecture exists to remove.

pta_tpaqcn_waveguide_pol.py is the replacement physics: a uniaxial film with
n_o in-plane and n_e out-of-plane, confinement from an asymmetric-slab
dispersion relation solved by bisection, and the effective-index method for the
second dimension.  With it, phase matching exists.  It also shows the
birefringence must sit inside a WINDOW of roughly 1.2x to 4.8x the material
dispersion: too little and the branches never cross, too much and the second
harmonic sits below the fundamental everywhere and then stops being guided.

pta_tpaqcn_measured.py checks the review against the device the proposal
needs, which the same group has already published (Theriault et al., Sci. Adv.
12, eaeg3170, May 2026): TE00 -> TM00 phase matching in a 230 nm TPA-QCN film,
29 %/W/cm^2 at 20 dB/cm.  Indices digitised from its figures put TPA-QCN inside
the window, so the premise holds.  Anchored on the measured efficiency, the
same activation costs 326 pJ in a 2 mm unit as built, and about 8 pJ in a 6 mm
unit with the device's avoidable leakage removed and the best derivative's
chi(2) -- still above O-E-O.  Two claims of the first draft are withdrawn: that
the film must be 0.4-2 um thick (the model's air-clad geometry was the limit,
not the film; under a low-index cover 230 nm is well guided), and that about
1 pJ is the honest design point (it used the assumed chi(2)).  The script is
standard library only and runs in seconds.

The review also records what the proposal does not see: removing the ADC
between layers removes the only requantisation point in the datapath, so analog
error compounds across depth with nothing to arrest it, amplified by the
activation's own slope at exactly its operating point.  And the measured device
adds a fixed-pattern error O-E-O never had: 14 nm of strip width halves a
unit's conversion at a shared pump wavelength.  Section 6 specifies the
experiment that settles both -- an S_ACT stage in the c930 NPU carrying a
CME-derived transfer table, the analog error model with per-unit detuning, and
a configurable O-E-O reset interval -- which needs no fab, no PDK and no
photonics.
